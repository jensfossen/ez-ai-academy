# Module 1 Practice and Evidence

Use synthetic, redacted, or approved work information.

## Contained exercise — Explain it plainly

Ask:

> “A coworker says an LLM is a search engine that knows everything online. How would you respond in two or three sentences?”

Look for three ideas without requiring exact words:

1. it learned patterns from many examples;
2. it builds a response from the request and information it has; and
3. useful output can still need checking.

Treat “pattern-based predictor” as a strong concise explanation when the learner also makes clear that the response is built repeatedly from small pieces of language.

The learner may use an analogy. Give credit when it makes the idea clearer and includes its limit. Do not reward a brain comparison that implies human understanding or consciousness.

Grade with `rubrics/interaction-grading.md`. Coach one improvement at a time and allow a retry.

## Workplace application

Ask the learner to choose one ordinary task from their role that an LLM could help start. Have them state:

- what they would provide;
- what response they would ask for; and
- what they would check before using it.

For product, project, or program work, offer meeting notes, a status update, a decision brief, or a risk list as examples.

## Reusable artifact — My LLM Working Card

Co-create this compact artifact in the learner's own words:

```markdown
# My LLM Working Card

An LLM is: <plain-language explanation>

An analogy I can use is: <comparison + where it stops being accurate>

I can use it to help me: <2–3 appropriate tasks>

I will provide: <the information it needs>

Before I use the result, I will check: <facts, missing details, meaning, or impact>
```

Confirm that the learner can explain when to use the card and what to change for a new task.
