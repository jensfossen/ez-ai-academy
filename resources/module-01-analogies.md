# Module 1 Analogy Cards

Use one or two cards at a time. An analogy is a bridge into the idea, not a complete technical definition. Always pair the helpful comparison with its limit.

## Recommended starting language

**An LLM is a pattern-based predictor. It learned patterns from many examples, then uses those patterns to predict the next small piece of a response—again and again—until it has built a useful answer.**

This is the default Module 1 mental model. It is more accurate than “predicts the next response,” which can imply that the whole answer appears in one prediction.

## Autocomplete grown up

**Simple version:** An LLM is like autocomplete expanded from finishing a few words to helping build whole responses.

**What it explains:** The model repeatedly predicts what should come next based on patterns it learned and the request in front of it.

**Where it breaks:** Ordinary autocomplete offers a short continuation. An LLM has learned much richer patterns, so it can reorganize, summarize, compare, draft, and respond to instructions. “Autocomplete” alone can make the capability sound too simple.

## A very fast drafting partner

**Simple version:** An LLM is like a very fast drafting partner that has studied many examples and can quickly produce a first version.

**What it explains:** It is useful for transforming language—drafting, summarizing, organizing, and exploring options.

**Where it breaks:** A human partner has lived experience, personal judgment, and accountability. An LLM does not. Its confident draft can include mistakes or unsupported details.

## A pattern-based predictor

**Simple version:** Imagine a system that has studied an enormous number of language patterns. You give it a starting point, and it predicts the next small piece of language repeatedly until it has built a response.

**What it explains:** The response is generated one piece at a time from learned patterns rather than copied from one stored answer.

**Where it breaks:** “Predictor” can sound like a crystal ball. It predicts likely language, not future events or guaranteed truth. The same request may also produce different wording.

## A careful brain comparison

**Simple version:** An LLM was loosely inspired by a simplified idea of many connected signals, somewhat like the way the brain has connected cells.

**What it explains:** Useful behavior can emerge from many small connections working together rather than from one giant list of hand-written rules.

**Where it breaks:** This is a distant analogy, not equivalence. A human brain learns through a body, relationships, memory, senses, goals, and lived experience. An LLM processes patterns in data. Do not say it thinks, understands, believes, or remembers like a person.

## Mentor prompt

After showing two analogies, ask:

> “Which comparison would work best for someone you work with—and what important limitation would you add?”

Accept a new learner-created analogy when it preserves the two central ideas: the LLM builds responses from learned patterns, and its output still needs human judgment.
